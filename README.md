## What I do

I’m a product person who likes hard, slightly messy problems.

The kind where product is not just screens and tickets, but the thing that connects:

* what users are actually trying to do
* how operations really work
* where data can improve decisions
* how the business makes money
* where AI can help without creating chaos

I’ve worked across AI platforms, marketplaces, healthcare commerce, edtech, logistics, surveys, and B2B/B2C/B2B2C products.

The common thread: **turning ambiguity into systems that scale.**

---

## How I think about product 🧠

I usually come back to a few questions:

* What is the real behavior underneath the feature request?
* What loop are we trying to create or improve?
* What part of the workflow is broken, slow, or invisible?
* What decision needs better data?
* Where should AI assist, and where should a human stay in control?
* What would make this useful enough to become a habit?

I like products that sit close to reality: supply, demand, fulfillment, pricing, routing, trust, quality, incentives, and repeat usage.

## Principles I keep using

| Principle                | What it means                                                                   |
| ------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Loops > launches**     | A launch is a moment. A loop compounds.                                         |
| **Reality > theory**     | Watch users, tickets, ops, sales calls, and edge cases.                         |
| **Clarity > cleverness** | If nobody can explain it simply, it is not ready.                               |
| **Judgment stays human** | AI can argue, draft, compare, and simulate. The decision is still ours.         |
| **Trust is designed**    | Especially in AI products. Evals, review, failure modes, and guardrails matter. |
| **Systems beat heroics** | The best product work reduces future chaos.                                     |
